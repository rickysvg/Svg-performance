# Unified Rules of MMA

This is a coaching note, not a commission ruling. The Association of Boxing Commissions revised the Unified Rules in July 2024 and asked commissions to implement them. Each commission adopts changes itself. Promotions add house rules. Check the rules meeting. For an El Paso card, that check includes Texas TDLR.

Elbow strikes are legal in pro MMA except to the spine or the back of the head. The illegal zone is the mohawk: from the crown, about an inch to either side, down the back of the head and the neck, and down the spine. The 12-6 elbow, a straight down elbow, stopped being an ABC foul in that July 2024 revision. Any arc was already legal. New York adopted the removal. Texas's published administrative code has still listed striking downward with the point of the elbow as a foul. Do not call 12-6 legal everywhere.

A grounded fighter, in the 2024 ABC text, has any body part other than the hands or feet on the canvas. Hands down alone do not make them grounded. You may not knee or kick a grounded fighter in the head. Older commissions may still use a palm-down or knee-down definition.

Fouls that show up in the clinch: grabbing the fence with fingers or toes, grabbing shorts or gloves, throat strikes and trachea grabs, fingers out toward the eyes, eye gouges, fish-hooking, headbutts, small-joint manipulation, groin attacks, and spiking someone on the head or neck. A throw with an arc is not, by itself, a pile-driver. Framing and pushing with the forearm on the jaw is not a strike. Ripping the elbow in is a strike.
