# IFMA and Thai rules

Muay Thai does not have one rule set. Traditional stadium Muay Thai usually allows punches, kicks, knees, elbows, sweeps, and a long clinch. Knees up the middle and elbows to legal targets are part of that sport. Clinch time is scored, not automatically broken.

IFMA amateur Muay Thai is stricter. Elbows are generally banned. Clinch time is limited and the referee breaks it. The scoring emphasis is often more on kicks. Headgear and shin guards show up in amateur settings. A technique that wins a round at Rajadamnern can be a foul, or at least a break, on an IFMA card.

Do not answer "are elbows allowed?" with a yes unless you have named the rule set. For IFMA amateur, the coaching answer is no. For traditional Thai rules, usually yes, except to illegal targets such as the back of the head or the groin, and still subject to the promotion. Local US amateur Muay Thai often follows a version of IFMA or a state combo ruleset. Check the card.
