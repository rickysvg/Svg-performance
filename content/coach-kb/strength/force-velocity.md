# Force-velocity

Force times velocity is power, and you cannot max both at once. A heavy squat moved slowly lives on the force end. A light medicine-ball throw or a fast shadow combo lives on the velocity end. The middle, a moderate load moved as fast as the athlete can move it, is where a lot of sporting power sits. Fighters need the whole curve. A gym that only grinds heavy fives gets strong and slow. A gym that only does band speed gets quick and easy to move.

For a striker, force shows up in the clinch, the shot, and the ability to hold a frame. Velocity shows up in the jab, the kick, and the exit. Train both in the week, not in the same set. Heavy work: main lifts, 0-2 RIR, loads in lbs, max 4 sets. Speed work: light enough that the rep does not slow down, stopped when it does. Accessories max 3 sets.

Do not invent the member's loads. If their plan or last logged set is in the prompt, use that number and say it came from their log. If it is missing, give the RIR and the set cap and tell them to keep the load they can own.

Beginners live closer to the middle and the light end until the positions are clean. They do not need a force-velocity lecture as their first week. They need a squat, a hinge, a push, a pull, and a return to class.
