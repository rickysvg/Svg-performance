# Round conditioning

A 3-minute or 5-minute round is mostly aerobic, with hard bursts the athlete can repeat. Train that shape. The last round should look like the first. Count the rounds. Do not replace them with one all-out finisher.

Bike repeats, bag rounds, and pad rounds are the tools. Stay inside the round length they will fight. Rest long enough that the next round is honest. If the hands drop or the stance falls apart, the round was too hard. Shorten it.

In a long camp, base conditioning is repeatable and a bit easier. Specific prep makes the intervals look like the fight. Peak keeps a little speed and cuts the volume. Fight week keeps a short sharp piece and drops the hard sparring.

Do not put the hardest conditioning on the same day as hard sparring unless a coach on the floor set that day.
