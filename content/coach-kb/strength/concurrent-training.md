# Concurrent training

Fighters lift and they practice the sport. Those interfere when both are smashed on the same day at the same intensity. The fix is priority and order, not quitting the weight room.

If the day is a hard sparring day, sparring is the priority. Lift earlier in the week, or later the same day only if the lift is easy skill and the sets stay inside the cap. Do not test a squat max before live rounds. If the day is a strength day, lift first, then technical work that does not turn into a war. Two to four lift days next to class is enough for most members. More is not a badge.

Main lifts: at most 4 sets. Accessories: at most 3. RIR on the plan. Lbs. A missed lift day is not paid back by doubling Thursday. Change the next session, do not invent a new program.

Signs the mix is too much: the sport skill is getting sloppy, sleep is worse, and loads that were 2 RIR are now misses. Pull a set or move the hard lift 48 hours away from the hard spar. Fight week is not the time to add a lift. The taper note covers that.

Beginners do the smaller version. One or two lifts they can repeat, plus class. They do not need a concurrent-training spreadsheet.
