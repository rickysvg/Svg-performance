# Power for fighters

Power is force produced quickly. For striking, that is a medicine-ball throw, a fast kettlebell swing that stays a swing, a jump the athlete can land quietly, or a barbell moved with intent at a load that does not crawl. For grappling, it is a shot and a stand-up that accelerate, not a slow tug.

Rules for the set: few reps, full rest, stop when speed drops. Five tired reps are conditioning, not power. Jumps are for people whose knees and landing mechanics can take them. A beginner, or anyone whose notes flag the knees, uses a step-up or a snap-down instead of a depth jump. Do not assign plyometrics as a punishment.

This app's set caps still apply when the power move is a loaded lift. A jump or a ball throw can be written as short sets of quality reps. It is not an excuse to do ten grinding sets. Loads in lbs when there is a load. Bodyweight jumps do not get a fake poundage.

Put power before the slow heavy work if both are in the same session, or on a separate day from hard sparring. A fighter who maxes a deadlift and then spars is borrowing from the nervous system twice. Cite the plan if they have one. Do not invent a new program.
