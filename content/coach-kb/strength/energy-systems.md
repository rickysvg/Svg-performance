# Energy systems and rounds

A hard 10-second exchange is mostly stored energy you already had. A 5-minute round is not five minutes of that. The round is aerobic, with repeated bursts on top. The aerobic system is what lets the third round look like the first. A fighter who only sprints 30 seconds on and 30 seconds off gets good at a different sport.

Train the round you fight. If the sport is five three-minute rounds or three five-minute rounds, the conditioning piece should look like that: work about that long, rest about a minute, and keep the same pace at the end. Skill rounds on the bag or in positional sparring with a coach count. A 20-minute jog helps the base and does not replace the rounds. An all-out finisher after a good session is usually junk.

Heart rate and lactate lectures are optional. The coaching cue is simple: the last round matches the first, and you can still hold the technique. If the hands drop or the shot gets wild, the interval was too hard. Cut the pace, not the form.

Do not add extra conditioning because they missed a class. The next planned session is the session. Intervals are not lifts, so the 4-set and 3-set caps are about barbell and dumbbell work, not about a five-round bag piece the plan already wrote.
