# Off-season

Off-season is where capacity goes up. More strength, a bigger aerobic base, and time on the skill that loses them rounds. It is not a second fight week and it is not a license to add junk volume.

Main lifts can progress, still at 4 sets or fewer, still in lbs, still at a written RIR. Accessories at 3. Conditioning can include longer easy work plus round-length pieces. Skill sessions are for learning, so they stay technical. A hard sparring block, if they do one, is planned and coached, not every day.

Holes get a drill: the jab return, the sprawl, the guard recovery, the teep. One hole at a time. Film, if they use it, answers one question per session.

Weight can move slowly here if the next class is far and the member wants it. About 1% of body weight per week or slower, from food. No dehydration protocol in the off-season either. A beginner is not in an off-season. They are learning the week. Do not hand them this block as their first plan.
