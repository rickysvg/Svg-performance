# Arousal control

Nerves mean the fight or the spar matters. They are not a character flaw. Performance sits on a curve: too flat and the jab never leaves, too high and the breathing locks and the eyes stop seeing. The job is to move one step toward the middle.

To downshift, make the exhale longer than the inhale. About four seconds in and six seconds out, a few rounds, shoulders dropped, jaw unclenched. Do it the night before and again in the locker room. To upshift when they feel frozen, stand taller, one sharp breath, and a cue word tied to an action: "jab" or "level." Then do that action once. Thinking about being calm is not the tool. The breath and the action are the tool.

Freezing in sparring is this problem, not a tactics essay. Settle the breath, then throw the one shot they already know. A game plan is a different conversation.

No shame. Do not call them soft. Do not tell them to ignore a headache or to spar through dizziness. Head symptoms stop the session. Arousal tools are for a healthy athlete who is keyed up, not for a possible concussion.
