# Taekwondo fundamentals

World Taekwondo sparring is a kicking game. The stance is narrow and tall enough to chamber fast, with the hands up because a kick is not a guard. A roundhouse chambers the knee, turns the hip, snaps the foot to the target, and puts the foot back down on a base that can move. People get swept or countered when the kick hangs in the air and the hands drop.

Head kicks score more than body kicks. Turning kicks score more than straightforward ones. A punch to the trunk can score. A punch to the head does not score in WT sparring, and elbows and knees are not part of the sport. Exact point values change. Check the current World Taekwondo rules instead of memorizing an old chart.

The standing leg pivots. If the foot stays glued, the hip cannot turn and the kick pushes instead of snapping. Recover the guard before the next kick. Footwork is small and constant so the chamber is a surprise.

Drill ten slow chambers each side with the foot returning to the floor under the hip, then one round of body round kicks to a paddle, then head-kick mechanics on a held target with a coach. No hard contact to a partner's head in a solo drill.
