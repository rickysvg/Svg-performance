# Boxing fundamentals

A boxing stance is a coil, not a lean. Feet about shoulder width, lead foot in, rear foot out enough to turn the hip. Weight stays near the balls of the feet so you can step. Chin down behind the lead shoulder. Both hands home: lead hand at cheekbone height, rear hand on the jaw. Elbows cover the ribs. Do not reach with the lead hand to measure distance. The jab does that.

The jab is a straight piston from the lead shoulder. Extend the fist, rotate it slightly at the end, and snap the hand back to the same spot on the face. If the hand comes back low, the counter is already on its way. The cross starts in the rear hip and rear foot, not the arm. Turn the hip, send the fist straight, and recover the hand and the hip together. A hook is a short turn with the elbow near the height of the fist. An uppercut comes up from the legs through a bent elbow, not a looping swing from the hip.

Footwork is step and slide. The lead foot moves first going forward. The rear foot moves first going back. Do not cross the feet. Head movement is a small exit off the line, then the hands come back. Shelling with the eyes shut is not defense.

Common mistakes: dropping the jab hand, leaning over the front knee to reach, crossing the feet on the exit, and holding the breath. Drill one round of jab only, one round of jab-cross with a full return to stance, then have a coach watch. Live sparring is a class decision, not a solo assignment.

Amateur boxing often uses headgear and a standing eight. Pro boxing is scored on the 10-point must system. Elbows and holding are fouls. The referee breaks the clinch. Do not teach clinch fighting as boxing.
