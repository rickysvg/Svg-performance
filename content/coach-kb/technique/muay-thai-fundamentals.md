# Muay Thai fundamentals

The Thai stance is taller and more square than a boxing stance so both legs can kick and check. Hands are up, chin down, and the lead hip is available. Weight stays balanced. If you sit too far back you cannot check. If you fall in you cannot kick.

The teep is a push kick, not a swing. Chamber the knee, keep the standing leg soft, push through the ball of the foot or the flat of the foot into the hip or belt line, and put the foot back down under you. Falling in means the standing heel came off and the hips chased the kick. The round kick turns the hip over and meets the target with the shin, not the foot. Check a kick with the shin lifted, not the thigh turned out and the toes pointed at the kicker.

In the clinch, get a collar tie or a bicep control, posture up, and off-balance before the knee. Knees travel up the middle. Elbows, when the rules allow them, are short and return to the guard. A long clinch without a score is stalling under many amateur rules.

Traditional Thai rules usually allow elbows, knees, sweeps, and a long clinch. IFMA amateur Muay Thai usually bans elbows and limits how long you can hold. Do not assume stadium rules on an amateur card. Drill the teep in pairs: ten slow reps, then one round where the only score is a teep that returns to stance. A coach watches before you add power.
