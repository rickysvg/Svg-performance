# Jiu-jitsu fundamentals

Frames before muscle. In closed guard, your feet are locked, your hips are in, and your hands frame the shoulder and the biceps so their posture stays broken. If they posture up, angle your hips to one side before you attack the arm. An armbar from closed guard: control the wrist, swing the leg across the face, the calf pinches the shoulder, the knees stay tight, the thumb points up, and the hips drive in. Extending the arm with your arms, while your hips stay far away, lets them stack you.

Bottom escapes start with a frame and a hip escape, not a bridge into a stronger person. Mount escapes need a frame on the hip and a trap of an arm or a foot. Side control needs a frame on the neck and the hip before the shrimp. On the back, a seatbelt and hooks come before the choke. A rear-naked choke is on the neck, with the choking arm's elbow under the chin. Squeezing the jaw is a crank, not the finish you want as a default.

IBJJF gi rules ban heel hooks at every belt and ban knee reaping. Slams are illegal. No-gi leg locks change by belt. Check the current IBJJF book. Tap early, and let go when they tap.

Drill the armbar as ten slow finishes from a partner who gives the posture break, then a partner who postures once. Stop before you wrench a straight arm.
