# Takedown defense

A shot is beaten before the hands lock. Hands on their head and shoulders, hips back, and a sprawl when the level changes. The sprawl is hips to the mat or to their back, legs back, not a dive forward onto your knees. Then a whizzer or a crossface, and you work back to your feet. On the cage, the same idea uses the fence: whizzer, head position, and a step to the side so you are not square and stuck.

If they finish and you are down, frames come in before a technical stand-up or a guard. Posture in their guard if you are the one on top, and do not reach an arm past their hip. In MMA the grounded-fighter rule matters: under the 2024 ABC text, a fighter with anything other than the hands or feet on the canvas may not be kneed or kicked in the head. Commissions that have not adopted that text may still use an older definition. Do not knee a downed opponent's head.

Wrestling and jiu-jitsu have no strikes. Do not add a knee to a wrestling sprawl drill.

Drill the sprawl to a whizzer in five slow reps, then a partner who shoots at half speed to a marked line. Stop the rep when the hips are down and the head is safe. A coach watches before the shot is live.
