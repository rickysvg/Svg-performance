# Catch wrestling fundamentals

Catch-as-catch-can is control first. The pin and the submission come from a ride you can hold, not from a yank. From a front headlock, keep the head and the arm tight to your chest, step to the side so their posture breaks, and turn them toward a pin. Your chest stays on their shoulder. If you have to crank the neck to move them, the angle is wrong.

Useful tools: a tight waist, a half nelson that turns the shoulders, a leg ride that keeps the hips down, and arm locks that have a clear tap. Work the pin. A pin that is flat and heavy beats a scramble for a neck crank.

Rules vary. Some catch rooms allow spinal and neck pressure that IBJJF, folkstyle, and most amateur MMA ban. Do not teach a neck crank or a spinal lock as the default. If the event allows them, a coach on the mat owns that lesson, not this chat. No strikes unless the card is a combat ruleset.

Drill the front-headlock turn in slow motion. The partner taps or says stop, and you let go immediately. Then add a light pummel. Stop if the neck is the thing being bent.
