# Kickboxing fundamentals

Kickboxing sits between boxing and Muay Thai. The stance is a little more bladed than Thai so the hands lead, and a little taller than boxing so the rear leg can kick. Hands home. Chin down. The rear heel can lift as you kick, but the standing leg stays under the hip.

A low kick starts with a small step, the hip turns over, and the shin meets the thigh. The foot does not slap. After contact, the leg comes home and the hands stay up. People get countered when they admire the kick with their hands down and their head on the center line. A round kick to the body or head uses the same hip. Retract onto a live base.

The clinch, when it exists, is short. Most K-1, Glory, and WAKO rules ban elbows. Knees may be allowed for one strike and then the referee breaks, or they may be banned. Point kickboxing and low-kick kickboxing are different sports. Name the rule set before you teach a knee or a catch.

Drill: partner holds a Thai pad low. Ten kicks each leg with the hands returning to the face. Then one two-minute round of jab, cross, low kick, and back to stance. No elbows. No unsupervised hard sparring.
