# MMA cage fundamentals

The fence is a tool. It is not a thing you grab. Under the Unified Rules, grabbing the fence with fingers or toes is a foul. A hand may push off. Toes may pass through the fence. It becomes a foul when the fingers or toes grab and control position. Texas's published text has been stricter, so on an El Paso card you check TDLR before you count on a push-off.

When you are pinned, win an underhook if you can. Frame the jaw or the collarbone with the forearm, elbow tight, bone on bone, and turn the head. Then step the hips off the fence at an angle. A straight arm gets dragged. A frame on the chest collapses. Fingers toward the eyes are a foul. Pressure on the windpipe can be read as a throat foul. A crossface on the jaw is a push, not an elbow strike, as long as you do not pull the elbow back and drive it in.

Pulling the elbow back and striking makes it an elbow. That strike is legal under pro Unified Rules except to the spine and the back of the head. Most amateur rules ban elbow and forearm strikes entirely. The ABC removed the purely vertical 12-6 elbow as a foul in the July 2024 revision. Not every commission has adopted that. Check the commission.

Drill: partner pins you on the wall for short rounds. Frame, turn the head, angle off. Cooperative, then half speed, then live positional with a coach. No unsupervised hard sparring. Do not grab the fence to stop a shot.
