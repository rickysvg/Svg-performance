# Reading a jab

A jab tells on itself. Watch the rear hand, the lead shoulder, and the weight. A jab that comes with the rear hand lazy is a counter target. A jab that comes with the head on the center line and the feet stuck is a step-outside target. You do not need ten reads. You need one that you can say during the round.

Counters that stay simple: slip outside and throw the cross, or parry and step in with the rear hand, then exit. In kickboxing or Muay Thai the same read can end in a low kick as they recover the jab, if that kick is legal. In pure boxing, do not add a kick. Hands come home after the counter. A counter with the hands down is how the second jab lands.

If-then: if the jab is a paw with no hip, counter down the middle. If the jab is long and they lean, step off and answer. If you miss the read, shell and reset. Do not stand still and think.

Film question: "What does their rear hand do when the jab leaves?" Ten jabs, one note. Then a drill round where you may throw only after you call the read. Fight IQ, not a mindset essay. No unsupervised hard sparring.
