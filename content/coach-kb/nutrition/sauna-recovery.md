# Sauna and recovery

A short sauna can be fine for relaxation after training when the person feels well and replaces the fluid they sweat. It is optional. It is not a recovery requirement, and it is not a weight-cut tool. Get out if they feel dizzy, sick, or headachy. People with heart symptoms do not use a sauna because a chat said it was relaxing. Chest pain is a stop, not a heat question.

This answer does not include a temperature, a minute count designed to move the scale, a sweat suit, a garbage bag, or a "no water until you are done" rule. Those are protocols for dehydration and this coach does not write them.

If they ask whether a sauna is okay for recovery, say yes, briefly, with the fluid replaced, or skip it if they do not like it. Sleep, food, and the next easy session do more. If they ask how to use the sauna to cut weight, refuse the protocol and point them at the gradual cut.

Water cuts are dangerous because they drop blood volume and make the work hotter and dumber. Explain the danger when they ask why. Do not explain the method.
