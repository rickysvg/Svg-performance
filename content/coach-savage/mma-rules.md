# Rules reference (general, not legal advice)

This file is a coaching reference, not a commission ruling and not legal advice. Promotions add their own rules. Each commission adopts changes itself. Always confirm at the rules meeting.

Sources checked October 14, 2026:

- Association of Boxing Commissions and Combative Sports, Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts, revised July 2024 (approved July 23, 2024; implementation requested November 1, 2024). https://www.abcboxing.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/unified-mma-rules-rev-july-2024.pdf
- New York Athletic Commission adoption of those two changes, effective November 1, 2024. https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/09/revised-unified-rules-of-mixed-martial-arts.pdf
- Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, 16 TAC §61.111, as published by Cornell LII (the downward-elbow foul is still in the published text). https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/16-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-61-111
- IMMAF fouls list (2025) and athlete eligibility. https://immaf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/IMMAF-FOULS-ALL.pdf and https://immaf.org/athletes/athlete-eligibility/

## Unified Rules of MMA (pro)

Elbow strikes are legal in pro MMA under the Unified Rules, except strikes to the spine or the back of the head. The ABC describes that illegal zone as the "mohawk": it starts at the crown with about a one-inch variance to either side, runs to the occipital junction, then covers the width of the neck (the nape) and continues down the spine with about a one-inch variance, including the tailbone.

The 12-6 elbow (a straight-down elbow, 12 o'clock to 6 o'clock) stopped being an ABC foul in the July 2024 revision. Any arc was already legal; the change removed the purely straight up-and-down elbow from the foul list. Adoption is not automatic. New York adopted the removal effective November 1, 2024, and repealed its matching foul for "striking downward using the point of the elbow." Texas's published administrative code, 16 TAC §61.111(s)(21), still lists "striking downward using the point of the elbow" as a foul. Always say: check the commission.

Grounded fighter, 2024 definition: a fighter is grounded, and may not be legally kneed or kicked to the head, when any body part other than the hands or feet is on the canvas. Hands down alone do not make the fighter grounded under this ABC text. Commissions that have not adopted the 2024 text may still use an older palm-down or knee-down definition. Check the commission.

Fouls that matter in the clinch and on the cage, from the July 2024 Unified Rules:

- Holding or grabbing the fence or ropes with fingers or toes. A hand may push off the fence. Toes may pass through the fence. It becomes a foul when fingers or toes grab the fence and control position. Texas's published text is stricter and lists "holding the fence or the ropes" as a foul, so do not assume a push-off is legal in Texas until TDLR says so.
- Holding the opponent's shorts or gloves.
- Throat strikes of any kind, including grabbing the trachea. A strike that happens to land on the throat in a stand-up exchange can still be ruled a legal blow. Digging the thumb or fingers into the neck or windpipe is a foul.
- Fingers outstretched toward the opponent's face or eyes.
- Eye gouging of any kind.
- Fish-hooking.
- Head butts.
- Small-joint manipulation.
- Groin attacks of any kind.
- Spiking an opponent onto the head or neck (a pile-driver). A throw with an arc is not, by itself, that foul.
- Timidity, including avoiding contact or stalling.

Framing and pushing — forearm or elbow pressure into the jaw, face, collarbone, or neck to make space or turn the head — is not listed as a strike and is legal under these foul lists. The hand still cannot gouge the eyes or point fingers at the face. Pressure on the windpipe can be read as a throat attack or a choke attempt, depending on how it is done. A crossface across the jaw is standard and legal. Pulling the arm back and driving the elbow in turns the frame into an elbow strike: legal under pro Unified Rules if it is not to the back of the head, and illegal under most amateur rules.

## Amateur MMA

The ABC amateur rules and IMMAF ban elbow and forearm strikes of any kind, and they ban knees to the head. IMMAF's 2025 fouls list also keeps heel hooks illegal, and it bans twisters, neck cranks, can-openers, cervical locks, and other submissions that put pressure on the neck or spine. Since 2025, IMMAF treats athletes who have competed in any MMA bout that allowed elbows or knees to the head as ineligible, with a grace period only for bouts that conflicting rules required before 2025.

Amateur striking rules vary by commission and by the local promotion. Do not tell a fighter that an elbow frame is a legal elbow strike just because pros may throw elbows.

## Other sports, one line each

- Muay Thai: most Thai rules allow elbows and clinch knees. Amateur and stadium rules still differ, so check the card.
- Kickboxing: most rule sets ban elbows and only allow a short clinch, if any.
- Boxing: elbows and clinching are both fouls. The referee breaks the clinch.

## Close

Promotions can add their own rules. Always confirm at the rules meeting. For an El Paso card, that check is Texas TDLR, not only the ABC PDF.
